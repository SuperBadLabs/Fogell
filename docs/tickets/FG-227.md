# FG-227 — fflat vs running the `.fsx` under `dotnet fsi`

Status: **DONE**. The question is answered by measurement: **compiling is
correct, by a factor of about eleven**, and the reason is not the one FG-226
gives for it. This ticket exists so nobody re-litigates the choice from the
intuition that looked obvious and was wrong.

## The question

FG-226 compiles eight `.fsx` sources to native binaries with fflat. The same
sources can be run directly by `dotnet fsi`. That option was never measured
before choosing, and it looks attractive on paper: `fsi` ships inside the .NET
SDK, which `actions/setup-dotnet` and `dotnet build` already make a hard
dependency, so it would cost **nothing** in dependency surface — against fflat's
929 MB store, version-only pin, brotli symlink repair, apphost-shim store
discovery, six-byte non-reproducibility, and one-maintainer risk on the blocking
path.

Every one of those fflat costs is real. They are simply not the deciding term.

## The answer

Measured on one quiet 32-core host, same tree, every engine checked to produce
**byte-identical output before being timed**:

| tool | invocations per gate run | fflat | `dotnet fsi` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `audit-stale-refs` | 92 | 6.26 s | 177.74 s |
| `audit-board-numbers` | 24 | 0.17 s | 26.23 s |
| `audit-claims` | 17 | 1.80 s | 23.78 s |
| `generate-scorecard` | 1 | 0.74 s | 2.62 s |
| run subtotal | 134 | 8.96 s | 230.37 s |
| compile, once | | 12.57 s | — |
| **total per gate run** | | **21.53 s** | **230.37 s** |

**fflat is 10.7× cheaper per gate run**: 21.5 s against 3 min 50 s.

Per-tool medians behind that table (3–15 runs each): `audit-stale-refs` 68 ms vs
1932 ms, `audit-board-numbers` 7 ms vs 1093 ms, `audit-claims` 106 ms vs 1399 ms,
`generate-scorecard --check` 736 ms vs 2615 ms. Do-nothing startup floor: native
**1 ms**, `dotnet fsi` **642 ms**.

`probe-input` was invoked 7 times by the fail-closed proof arms and is excluded
from the totals — there is no fflat baseline for it that is comparable, because
its runtime is dominated by HTTP timeouts rather than by the engine.
`sync-scm-cases` is not measured at all: exercising it pushes to the fixture
remote. That is the same evidence boundary FG-226 states for those two tools.

## Why — the term nobody had counted

**The gate invokes these tools 134 times, not once.** `audit-stale-refs` alone
runs **92 times**, because the proof harnesses drive it per mutant and per
fixture. `fsi` recompiles the script on every one of those invocations; fflat
compiles eight tools once and reuses them.

- `fsi` extra cost per invocation: **1652 ms**
- break-even against the 12.57 s build: **7.6 invocations**
- actual: **134** — about **18× past break-even**

The conclusion is not sensitive to measurement error, and the check has to be
run in the ADVERSARIAL direction to mean anything. An earlier draft said "halve
every fflat timing and double every fsi one", which perturbs in the direction
that FAVOURS the conclusion — it yields 42.8×, not the 5× claimed, and proves
nothing. Perturbed against the conclusion instead — DOUBLE every fflat timing
and HALVE every fsi one — compiling still wins by **2.67×**. Either perturbation
alone gives 5.35×.

## What FG-226's own justification gets wrong

FG-226 justifies the port on PER-INVOCATION SPEED. That is the weaker argument
and it is partly overstated: its `audit-stale-refs` figure is engine PLUS 32
cores, because the `.fsx` uses `Parallel.ForEach` where the `.bb` original had no
parallelism primitive at all, and it would shrink on a 4-core hosted runner. The
honest engine-only comparison is `audit-claims`.

The real justification is **amortisation across a proof-heavy gate**. A gate that
runs its checkers in loops multiplies any compile-per-invocation cost by the loop
count. That argument is robust to core count, to workload size, and to the
engine timings being somewhat off — and it does not appear anywhere in FG-226.

## A blocker the fsi route would also have had to clear

Recorded because it is a fail-open, and cheap for a future reader to trip over:
`audit-board-numbers`, `audit-claims` and `generate-scorecard` locate the
repository with `Path.GetDirectoryName(Environment.ProcessPath)`. Under `fsi`
that is `/usr/lib/dotnet/dotnet`, so the root resolves to `/usr/lib`, the tools
scan nothing, and **they still exit 0** — `audit-claims` reported `0 source files
scanned` and returned success. Deriving from `__SOURCE_DIRECTORY__` fixes it and
restores byte-identical output on all four re-checked tools.

## Correcting FG-226's cost table

FG-226 states a net gate cost of **+8.42 s**. Re-measured on the merged tree:

| step | figure |
|---|---:|
| saved by faster audits (see note) | −19.4 s |
| `build-audits.sh` | +12.57 s |
| `prove-fsx-prelude.sh` (new lane) | +9.60 s |
| `prove-fg226-audit-tools.sh` (new lane) | +22.38 s |
| `prove-stale-refs.sh` 2.92 s → 17.42 s | +14.50 s |
| **net** | **≈ +39.7 s** |

The `−19.4 s` row is the one term here that is NOT decomposed and not
re-measurable from the merged tree as it stands: it is the babashka-vs-native
delta over the three gate audits, and the `.bb` originals are deleted. FG-226
states `−21.85 s` for the same three. The difference does not change the sign or
the order of the net, but its provenance is UNRECOVERED — it matches no
figure anywhere in the tree, and FG-226 states −21.85 s for the same three
audits — so it is marked rather than presented as measured. The whole-table sum, this row included,
checks: −19.4 + 12.57 + 9.60 + 22.38 + 14.50 = 39.65.

Its six-row table omits `prove-fg226-audit-tools.sh` entirely — a 22.4 s lane the
port itself introduced — and its `prove-fsx-prelude` row predates that proof's
seventh arm. **The port made the gate about 40 s slower per run, not 8.**

That is not an argument against the port. The ~32 s of new proof lanes is what
buys the exhaustive 65 536-code-unit differential and the fail-closed arms, and
those found real defects. It is an argument for the ticket saying so.

## Method, including two measurement failures worth recording

Timings are `perf_counter` around `subprocess.run`, medians, on a host at load
~1. Invocation counts are EMPIRICAL: each binary in `scripts/bin/` was replaced
by a wrapper that logged its own name and `exec`ed the real binary, and the
gate's ten tool-using lanes were then run.

**Two attempts produced clean-looking numbers that were not measurements.**

The first `fsi` timing reported 1196 ms for `audit-claims` and was cited as
evidence that `fsi` was viable. It was timing the `ProcessPath` defect above —
a tool that scanned zero files and exited 0. The number was wrong and the
conclusion drawn from it was wrong.

The first invocation count returned zero for every tool. The wrappers had been
installed in `scripts/bin/`, and `build-and-test.sh` runs `build-audits.sh` at
line 50, which **rewrites exactly those files** — the instrumentation was
destroyed before any audit ran, and an empty log is indistinguishable from "no
invocations" unless you check whether the instrument survived. It had not: the
wrapper was an ELF binary.

Both failures share a shape: **a measurement returning a suspiciously tidy result
because the thing being measured was not running.** Verify the instrument before
believing the reading.

## What this ticket does NOT claim

- Anything about a 4-core hosted runner. Every number is from one 32-core host,
  and the `Parallel.ForEach` component of the `audit-stale-refs` figure would
  largely disappear there. **THE DIRECTION SURVIVES; THE 10.7× DOES NOT.** Both
  engines run the same `Parallel.ForEach`, so losing cores adds roughly the same
  ABSOLUTE time to each total, and adding a constant to both sides of a ratio
  above 1 moves it toward 1. Under the most pessimistic assumption — all 68 ms of
  each `audit-stale-refs` invocation perfectly parallel, scaling 8× on 4 cores,
  so ≈ +45.8 s on both sides — the ratio becomes (230.37 + 45.8) / (21.53 + 45.8)
  ≈ **4.1×**. That is a FLOOR: less-than-perfect parallelism, or fsi's own
  compile slowing too, both push it back up. An earlier revision of this bullet
  said the 10.7× survives, which is wrong, and it survived one round longer than
  the board row because the correction was applied to the row and not here.
- Any measurement of `sync-scm-cases`, or an engine-comparable one of
  `probe-input`.
- That fflat's costs are acceptable. They are enumerated at the top and unchanged
  — 929 MB, a version-only pin weaker than the SHA256 it replaced, a brotli
  repair, shim-parsing store discovery, no reproducibility, one maintainer. This
  ticket says only that they buy something that measures at **209 s per gate
  run**, and that the alternative is worse, not that the price is right.
